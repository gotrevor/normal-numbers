## 🧠 Context to carry forward

The mental model that made this tractable — worth keeping if anyone revisits this corner:

* **Everything factors through one density lemma.**  Both non-normality and abelian normality
  are statements about events that depend only on `(n % 4, c(n/4) … c(n/4+S-1))`.  So the whole
  analytic content is a single workhorse (`tendsto_blockEvent`), and both headline proofs become
  finite combinatorics on top of it.  Do not re-derive limits per statement.
* **The abelian side is NOT a `decide`.**  The kickoff's hint ("finite core") is misleading if
  read as "enumerate": the sum runs over `16^(L+1)` hex words for arbitrary `L`.  The real proof
  is a strong induction on `L` peeling the *leading* hex digit, with Chu-Vandermonde
  (`Nat.add_choose_eq`) gluing the block-suffix Binomial to the recursive tail.  That is the only
  route I found and it is clean; don't go looking for a `decide`.
* **Considered and not needed**: the character route via `isAbelianNormalTwo_iff_symParityMean`
  (the kickoff's "alternative").  The distribution route is more direct once you have the
  interval-Binomial fact, because `∑ l_t = L` makes the product of per-block generating factors
  collapse immediately.  I also considered `Polynomial ℚ` generating functions; the ℕ-valued
  `wordCount` recursion is strictly lighter and avoids coefficient extraction.
* **`hexSwap` is not a bijection** (2 and 3 both map to 3) — that is exactly why the block law is
  non-uniform and the sequence is non-normal.  If a future variant "simplifies" the substitution
  into a permutation, the whole construction collapses to uniform.
* **Why `AbelianIntervalBinomial` is parametrized by a generic `g`**: `hexSwap` is *defined* in
  `AbelianBinaryExample.lean`, so a helper file cannot depend on it.  Parametrizing by the
  substitution and taking `HBinom g` as a hypothesis breaks the cycle and keeps the combinatorics
  reusable for any other block substitution someone wants to test.
