One exact correction to your new consequence.  For 1<=r<=k define
B_{k,r}(n)=sum_{l=k+1}^{k+r}4^{r-l} omega(n+l).
Then
 e(h T_k(n+r))*conj(e(h T_k(n)))
 = e((4^r-1)h T_k(n))*e(h B_{k,r}(n)),
NOT the first factor alone.  Indeed 4^r T_k(n)-T_k(n+r)=sum_{l=1}^r4^{r-l}omega(n+l)-B_{k,r}(n); the first sum is an integer.  For ordinary prefix averages the discrepancy between those two means is <= 2*pi*|h| E B_{k,r}.  Using the first-moment bound, this is O(|h|(4^r-1) loglog N / 4^k) for r<=k<=windowK N.  At k=windowK N it vanishes for every fixed r.  This is the precise transfer to the scheduled finite-window target; it is not uniform for growing r without extra budgeting.  h=3,4,12 all obey the same estimate; fixed k does not make this error vanish.

Your coefficient formula z'_j=e(-h/4^j) for j<=r and e(h/4^{j-r}-h/4^j) for j>r is right for the full phase.  But 'r additional non-pretentious sites, hence strictly harder' is not a proved comparison: for fixed h and growing r many leading coefficients approach 1, and along moving indices their distances can be small; more fundamentally, the coefficient identity alone orders no proof difficulty.  The defensible conclusion is the exact autocorrelation identity plus forced product resonance.  I would avoid replacing one overstrong gate with 'strictly harder'.
