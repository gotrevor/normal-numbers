Subject: Exact centered-in-probability wiring; why centering alone misses the density-zero regime

Yes: a weaker wiring lemma is immediate, including arbitrary deterministic centering.  Let T_N(n) be the full tail, A_N(n) the truncated head, R_N=T_N-A_N, and c_N any real sequence.  Suppose for every eta>0,
  (1/N) #{n<N : dist(R_N(n)-c_N, Z)>eta} -> 0.
If the head Fourier mean tends to zero for each fixed nonzero integer h, the full Fourier mean also tends to zero.  No convergence or smallness of c_N is needed.

Exact proof: put B_N(eta) for that exceptional proportion.  Since h is integral and |exp(2*pi*i*h*a)-exp(2*pi*i*h*b)| <= 2*pi*|h|*dist(a-b,Z),
  |Mean e(h T_N) - e(h c_N) Mean e(h A_N)|
    <= 2 B_N(eta) + 2*pi*|h|*eta.
Take limsup in N, then eta down to zero.  Real-distance concentration is a stronger sufficient assumption; centered L1 convergence implies it as well.  This gives a concrete replacement consumer, but does not by itself establish the needed concentration.

Why I would not launch implementation yet: centering plus the usual square-root fluctuation scale only changes the required window from 4^J >> S to 4^J >> sqrt(S).  In the proposed bad regime write t=L2(N), v=loglog(t)=L4(N), S~t/v and ell=log(1/epsilon).  Either window requirement still gives J >= c log(t), hence log J >= v-O(1).  Even granting constants C1,C2 bounded above AND below by positive constants, vanishing sieve error forces J^2 epsilon ->0, hence ell-2log J ->infinity.  If the regular-density example has fresh mass I >= c ell/v uniformly over the admissible epsilon window, the frozen fresh term is >=c' ell/sqrt(v), which diverges.  Thus the square-root centered tail does not fix this example.

For the uniform I lower bound, it must be proved for an actual regularly thinned prime set; it does not follow from an upper density envelope alone.  With a genuine asymptotic counting law pi_P(x)~pi(x)/L4(x), Abel summation over [N^epsilon,N] is the appropriate step.  Until that is supplied, label the calculation a conditional growth-regime obstruction, not a constructed counterexample.

Also please soften 'a proof needs a different tail treatment' to 'the current correlation majorant and tail criterion cannot both vanish in this regime.'  A sharper head/tail correlation argument is another logical possibility; failure of a sufficient majorant does not prove a theorem-level necessity.
