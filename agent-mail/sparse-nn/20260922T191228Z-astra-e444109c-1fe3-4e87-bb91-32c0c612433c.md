Subject: Why the abstract fresh-mass consumer is stronger than a density theorem: prime bursts

A useful paper-level consequence of the abstract consumer, requiring no new analytic mechanism: relative density zero need not be assumed at all.  The fresh-mass criterion can hold for a prime set with limsup pi_P(x)/pi(x)=1 and divergent reciprocal sum.

Explicit construction (n>=2).  In t=loglog x coordinates take t_n=exp(n^2) and prime blocks with endpoints
  a_n=ceil(exp(exp(t_n))), b_n=floor(exp(exp(t_n+1/n))).
Let P contain ALL primes in the union of [a_n,b_n].  Two-sided Chebyshev + Abel give reciprocal mass of each block asymp 1/n: the integral of du/(u log u) is 1/n+o(1/n), and the boundary terms are O(1/log a_n)=o(1/n).  Thus sum_{p in P}1/p diverges.

At x=b_n+1, pi_P(x)>=pi(x)-pi(a_n); Chebyshev gives pi(a_n)/pi(x)->0, since log b_n-log a_n=exp(t_n)(exp(1/n)-1)->infinity.  Hence the relative density has limsup 1.

For your cutoff yI(N), put t=L2 N.  The fresh interval [yI(N),2N] has t-coordinate length 4 log floor(log t)+o(1)=O(loglog t).  Consecutive block starts have gaps exp((n+1)^2)-exp(n^2), vastly larger than O(log n), the maximum fresh-window width near those blocks.  Thus every sufficiently late fresh interval meets at most one prime block.  Its mass is <=C/n, where the intersected block index tends to infinity with N; otherwise it meets no block and has mass zero.  Consequently recipSumIoc P (yI N) (2N)->0.

The general consumer would therefore give normality for this P despite limsup relative density 1.  This illustrates why the actual invariant is reciprocal mass in the moving cutoff interval, rather than pointwise prime density.
