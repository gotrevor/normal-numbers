Answer to your question: I do not have an estimate for the centered tail beating the sqrt(S) scale, and I think none exists for the tail alone.  With digits omega_P(n+j+1) (unbounded, mean ~S, variance ~S by Turan-Kubilius-type reasoning), the centered tail's fluctuation at scale sqrt(S)/4^J is the truth, so any criterion that bounds the tail by itself lands on 4^J >> sqrt(S) at best.

The candidate mechanism that escapes the regime is head/tail DECORRELATION, not tail size.  Exact identity: Mean e(hT_N) = Mean[e(hA_N) e(hR_N)].  Truncate the tail at level M with 4^(-M) S_P(2N) -> 0 (this part is the existing L1 argument, and M ~ log_4 S is harmless because nothing requires a correlation bound at level M).  Then
  |Mean e(hT_N)| <= |Mean e(hA_N)| * |Mean e(hR_M)| + |Cov(e(hA_N), e(hR_M))| + o(1)
                 <= |Mean e(hA_N)| + |Cov| + o(1).
The first term is the existing head bound (needs only exp(-fresh mass) -> 0 and the sieve term, with J free of S).  The second is a covariance between a function of the radical state of window n+1..n+J and a function of the state of the DISJOINT window n+J+1..n+J+M.  In the state model a prime p > J+M hits at most one position of the combined window, so cross-window dependence comes only from (a) primes p <= J+M in P, whose total mass sum_{p<=J+M, p in P} 1/p is O(1) at worst and o(1) for sparse P since J+M ~ L3, and (b) the residue factor mod primorial(J+M), handled exactly as now.  Under the model, that covariance should be controlled by the coupling mass, NOT by e^{4(J+M)} times a fresh-mass square root; if the majorant carries an e^{4k}-type factor the L4 regime kills it again, so that is the referee question for this mechanism.

What this would replace: TailOK (the 4^J >> S criterion) by a decorrelation hypothesis, and the wiring lemma would consume |Mean e(hA)| -> 0 plus Cov -> 0 instead.  The head bound already proved (window_bound_regime) is unchanged.  The new Lean object would be a two-window joint law: our JointState/empLaw machinery over the window of length J+M already gives the model law; what is new is the product-vs-joint comparison for the two sub-windows, i.e. a statement that the model expectation of f(head state) g(tail state) minus the product is bounded by the coupling mass.
