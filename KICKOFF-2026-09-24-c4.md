## The mathematics

`probes/abelian_window_sets.py` (read its docstring): near the uniform measure, the tangent space
of stationary laws on `{0,1}^k` is cut by the linear maps `A_L` (count-law deviation at length
`L`).  For `k = 6, 8` the realizable exact sets are exactly `∅` and those containing 1.  A generic
small perturbation inside `W_S = ∩_{L∈S} ker A_L` has full support, so an order-(k−1) Markov chain
realizes it, and a deterministic "typical" concatenation gives a sequence.

1. **Necessity first** (`abelianAt_one_of_abelianAt`): the mean of the length-`L` one-count is
   `L · (density of ones)` up to `O(L/N)` boundary terms, and Binomial(`L`,1/2) has mean `L/2`.
   Elementary; do this lap 1.
2. **Finite `S`**: realize a stationary word law on `{0,1}^k` by an explicit deterministic sequence
   whose `k`-block frequencies converge to it (de Bruijn-style / Markov typical concatenation).
   Consider whether it is cleaner to avoid Markov chains: pick a rational law and a periodic-ish
   block construction; `AbelianBinaryExample.lean` (hexSwap over a normal base-16 sequence) is a
   worked instance of "block law ⇒ sequence".
3. **Infinite `S`**: a limit of perturbations.  Stage `k` fixes the lengths `≤ k`; the depth-`k+1`
   correction must be small enough not to disturb decided lengths and must keep undecided `L ∉ S`
   violated.  The difficulty is uniformity: a sequence built stage by stage must have limiting
   frequencies for EVERY `L` simultaneously.  Standard approach: concatenate blocks realizing stage
   `k` laws over rapidly growing lengths (as in Champernowne / normal-number constructions), and
   make sure each non-member `L ∉ S` has a violation that persists (bounded away from 0) in the
   limit — or else stop and state exactly why it cannot.
4. Probes in `probes/` (stdlib-only Python, no PyPI in the box), each with a hand-computed
   known-answer check.
