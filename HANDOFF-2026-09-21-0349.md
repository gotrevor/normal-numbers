## 🧠 Context to carry forward

**The reformulation that made it tractable.** The paper states admissibility over the
decreasing list `p₁ > … > p_r`. Do **not** formalize sorted lists. We used rank-from-the-top,
`ab E q = #{p ∈ E | q < p}`, so "`q` is at an even position" is `Odd (ab E q)` and the position
is `(ab E q + 1)/2`. This is local, decidable, and — load-bearing — depends only on the part of
`E` weakly *above* each element, which is exactly what lets a first-failure prefix be cut off
(`ab (E.filter (q₀ ≤ ·)) q = ab E q` for `q ≥ q₀`).

**Three things the formalization taught that the assessment did not know.**

1. **(2) and (3) are ONE argument.** `sum_not_adm_eq` — the first-failure decomposition — is
   weight-generic. `f = 1` gives the pointwise minorant; `f = g` gives `defect_eq`, the exact
   nonnegative model defect. The paper presents them as two separate calculations.
2. **The factorial/Stirling step is unnecessary.** `xⁿ eₙ(W) ≤ ∏(1+x g) ≤ exp(x ∑g)` (that is
   `Finset.prod_add` plus `1+u ≤ eᵘ`), optimised at `x = n/T`, gives `eₙ ≤ (eT/n)ⁿ` directly —
   exactly what the paper extracts from `n! ≥ (n/e)ⁿ`. No factorial occurs anywhere in the file.
3. **The floor in the cutoffs is free.** For naturals `q0 > ⌊t⌋ ↔ q0 > t`. So the support side
   uses `⌊t⌋ ≤ t` and the error side uses `q0 > t`, with no loss on either. A cutoff falling
   below 2 is absorbed by the hypothesis's `max 2 t`, not a special case: there
   `log 2 > log t_ℓ = α^ℓ log y`, so the ratio bound holds *more* easily.
