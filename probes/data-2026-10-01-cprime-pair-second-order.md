# PairSecondOrder measured: |T2| / Fw² (2026-10-01)

Instrument: `experiments/cprime_fresh_cancellation.py --report pair --logN 20 22 24 --h 1 3 5 --a 1 0.5`
(test `test_pair_second_order_ratio_exact`, hand-derived, P = {101, 103}).

`T2 = W − W_y − T1`, where the probe's `T1 = mean[Φ_y·Σ_j (z_j^{fresh_j} − 1)]` is the Lean `T1'`
(`freshOneSite`).  So `T2` is exactly the left side of `PairSecondOrder`.  `Fw = Σ_j |z_j − 1|·S_P(y_j, N)`
is the weighted fresh mass, the finite-N form of `freshBudget`.

**Summary.**
- `|T2|/Fw² ∈ [0.007, 0.53]` over every set, h, a and N.  `|T2|/ρ²` spans `[0.22, 39.6]` and grows
  with h, so the `|w_j|`-weighting is the right scale and absorbs the h-dependence.
- Stable in N: for example `1 mod 5`, h = 1, a = 1 gives 0.229, 0.227, 0.226.
- The largest values come from the sparsest classes (`1 mod 61`: 0.37–0.53), and they **fall** with N
  (h = 1, a = 1: 0.469 → 0.403 → 0.369).
- At fixed N the ratio rises with q: h = 1, a = 1, 2^24 gives 0.18 (q = 3), 0.23 (5), 0.26 (13), 0.29 (23),
  0.30 (31) and 0.37 (61).  So uniformity in q is supported but not settled.  A `1/log N` approach from
  above (the first prime of a sparse class is large) fits this, but it is not proved.
- Not the proof regime: a ≥ 0.5, J = 5.
