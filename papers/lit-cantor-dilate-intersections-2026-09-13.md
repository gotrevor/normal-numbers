# Literature notes: K_d ∩ λ·K_d for rational λ (missing-digit Cantor sets and their rational dilates)

Date of search: 2026-09-13.  Instruments: WebSearch, WebFetch (arXiv abs / ar5iv / arXiv HTML), OpenAlex API (curl), zbMATH Open API (curl), OEIS (curl), pdftotext on two PDFs WebFetch saved.  Springer, ScienceDirect, JSTOR, Cambridge Core, Semantic Scholar (429) and zbMATH's HTML front end all refused direct fetches; where a paper's own page could not be opened I say so and name the surrogate actually read.

Conventions used below.  K_d = K_{g,D} with D = {0..g−1} \ {d}, i.e. reals in [0,1] whose base-g expansion avoids d.  "K ∩ λK as subsets of [0,∞)" = {x ∈ K : x/λ ∈ K}.  "mod 1" = {x ∈ K : frac(x/λ) ∈ K} or equivalently the union of the integer-translate fibres.  The p-adic literature (Abram–Lagarias) uses a third convention: p-adic integers whose expansion (infinite to the LEFT) avoids a digit; carries there propagate from the least significant digit, so it models the INTEGER question, not the real one.

## 1.  Real line: dim(K_d ∩ λK_d) for rational λ

### 1.1  Jiang, Li, Li, Wu (2026), "Dimension drop for intersections of Cantor sets"
- arXiv:2607.19813, submitted 2026-07-22.  Opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19813 and https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19813 .
- Abstract (verbatim): "Let E ⊂ ℝ be a self-similar set generated by a homogeneous iterated function system Φ with contraction ratio ρ ∈ (0,1).  Assume that Φ satisfies the open set condition and dim_H E < 1.  Let f be a C¹-diffeomorphism on ℝ.  We prove that if log|f'(x)|/log ρ ∉ ℚ for every x ∈ E ∩ f⁻¹(E), then the upper Minkowski dimension of f(E) ∩ E is strictly less than the Hausdorff dimension of E.  We also establish a quantitative dimension drop result when E is a missing-digit set and f is an affine map with rational slope satisfying a certain arithmetic condition.  Based on these results and a result of Shmerkin [Ann. of Math., 2019], we obtain characterizations of γ in various contexts such that dim̄_M((γE+α) ∩ E) < dim_H E for every α ∈ ℝ."
- Definitions (quoted from the HTML): "The missing-digit set K_{b,D} with base b and digit set D is defined by K_{b,D} = {∑_{j≥1} ε_j/b^j : ε_j ∈ D for all j ≥ 1}."  "We say that E is symmetric if E = c − E for some c ∈ ℝ."
- Theorem 1.4 (as extracted from the HTML): base b ≥ 3, 2 ≤ #D < b, γ = (s/r)·b^ℓ with r ∈ ℕ, ℓ ∈ ℤ, s ∈ ℤ\{0}, |s| ≠ r, gcd(s,r) = gcd(sr,b) = 1.  For any q ∈ ℕ with q > log(|s|+r)/log(b/#D) + 1, "dim̄_M((γK_{b,D}+α) ∩ K_{b,D}) ≤ log((#D)^q − 1)/(q log b)", a bound "independent of the exponent ℓ and the translation α".  It applies at α = 0, i.e. to K ∩ γK.
- Corollary 1.6 (quoted): when b is prime and K_{b,D} is symmetric, "dim̄_M((γK_{b,D}+α) ∩ K_{b,D}) < dim_H K_{b,D} for every α ∈ ℝ if and only if γ ∉ {±b^k : k ∈ ℤ}".
- Prior-work paragraph (quoted from the HTML): "Much work has been devoted to the size of f(E) ∩ E when f is affine.  Let γ, α ∈ ℝ with γ ≠ 0.  The measure of the intersection (γE+α) ∩ E has been investigated in [5].  According to [5, Theorem 4.5], if E satisfies SSC, then (γE+α) ∩ E has positive μ-measure only when γE+α contains an elementary piece of E.  Then by the Logarithmic Commensurability Theorem of Feng and Wang [8], this happens only when log|γ|/log ρ ∉ ℚ."  ([5] = Elekes–Keleti–Máthé, [8] = Feng–Wang; the last "∉" reads as a transcription slip for "∈", the theorem being that positive measure forces commensurability.)
- Lower-bound example (quoted): "it is easily checked that 4C ∩ C contains the self-similar set generated by the IFS {x/9, (x+8)/9}, which has Hausdorff dimension log2/log9.  Hence, dim̄_M(4C ∩ C) ≥ log2/log9 > max{2log2/log3 − 1, 0}."  (C = middle-third set.)
- The HTML extraction reports NO mention of finite automata, sofic sets, transfer matrices or spectral radii, and no general lower bound or positivity criterion.
- Relevance: this is the closest paper to item 1.  It settles the UPPER side for rational γ coprime to the base (strict drop below dim K, with an explicit quantitative bound) and gives one concrete positive example (4C ∩ C), but says nothing about when dim(K ∩ γK) > 0 in general.

### 1.2  Elekes, Keleti, Máthé (2010), "Self-similar and self-affine sets: measure of the intersection of two copies"
- Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 30 (2010), no. 2, 399–440.  arXiv:0704.3727.  Opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.3727 , https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0704.3727 .
- Measure-topology theorem (quoted from ar5iv): "For every g ∈ 𝒢 we have μ(K ∩ g(K)) > 0 ⟺ int_K(K ∩ g(K)) ≠ ∅" (for self-similar K with the strong separation condition, 𝒢 a group of similitudes/affine maps).
- Relevance: gives the measure-level statement quoted by [1.1]; for rational λ ≠ ±3^k, C ∩ λC has zero H^{log2/log3}-measure.  Says nothing about positivity of dimension.

### 1.3  Feng, Wang (2009), "On the structures of generating iterated function systems of Cantor sets"
- Adv. Math. 222 (2009), 1964–1981.  DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2009.06.022.  Opened: OpenAlex record and zbMATH review Zbl 1184.28013 via https://api.zbmath.org/v1/document/_search?search_string=ti:"generating iterated function systems of Cantor sets" .
- zbMATH review (quoted): "They prove that if dim_H F < 1, then all generating IFSs must have logarithmically commensurable contraction factors.  From this result, the authors derive a structure theorem for the semi-group of generating IFSs under open set condition."
- Relevance: the Logarithmic Commensurability Theorem used in [1.1] to rule out positive measure for rational λ ∉ ±3^ℤ.

### 1.4  Feng, Huang, Rao (2014), "Affine embeddings and intersections of Cantor sets"
- J. Math. Pures Appl. (2014).  arXiv:1406.5318.  Opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.5318 , https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1406.5318 .
- Abstract (verbatim): "Let E, F ⊂ ℝ^d be two self-similar sets.  Under mild conditions, we show that F can be C¹-embedded into E if and only if it can be affinely embedded into E; furthermore if F can not be affinely embedded into E, then the Hausdorff dimension of the intersection E ∩ f(F) is strictly less than that of F for any C¹-diffeomorphism f on ℝ^d."
- Definition (quoted): "For p ∈ ℕ with p ≥ 2, a Cantor p-set is the attractor of an IFS {x/p + a_i}_{i=1}^ℓ on ℝ, where {a_i : 1 ≤ i ≤ ℓ} is a proper subset of {0,1,…,p−1} containing at least two digits."  (Our K_d is a Cantor g-set.)
- Conjecture 1.2 (quoted): "Suppose that E, F are totally disconnected and F can be affinely embedded into E.  Then for each 1 ≤ j ≤ m, there exist non-negative rational numbers t_{i,j} such that β_j = ∏_{i=1}^ℓ α_i^{t_{i,j}}."
- Theorem 1.4(i) (quoted): "If α < 1/4, then C_β can be affinely embedded into C_α if and only if log β/log α ∈ ℕ."  Also (quoted): "if C_β can be affinely embedded into C_{1/3}, then 1/β should be an integer power of 3."
- Furstenberg's conjecture as stated there (quoted): "Furstenberg conjectured that if p, q are not powers of the same integer (i.e., log q/log p ∉ ℚ), then dim_H(A ∩ f(B)) ≤ max{0, dim_H A + dim_H B − 1}, where A is an arbitrary Cantor p-set and B a Cantor q-set, f is any affine map on ℝ."
- Relevance: same-base rational dilations are exactly the case EXCLUDED from Furstenberg's conjecture; the paper's Cantor-set results are about embeddings (λC + t ⊂ C forces λ = 3^{−k}), i.e. again the upper side.

### 1.5  Feng, Xiong (2018), "Affine embeddings of Cantor sets and dimension of αβ-sets"
- Israel J. Math. (2018).  arXiv:1609.05784.  Opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05784 .
- Abstract (verbatim): "Let E, F ⊂ ℝ^d be two self-similar sets, and suppose that F can be affinely embedded into E.  Under the assumption that E is dust-like and has a small Hausdorff dimension, we prove the logarithmic commensurability between the contraction ratios of E and F."

### 1.6  Algom (2016), "Affine embeddings of Cantor sets on the line"
- arXiv:1607.02849.  Opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02849 .
- Abstract (verbatim): "Let s ∈ (0,1), and let F ⊂ ℝ be a self similar set such that 0 < dim_H F ≤ s.  We prove that there exists δ = δ(s) > 0 such that if F admits an affine embedding into a homogeneous self similar set E and 0 ≤ dim_H E − dim_H F < δ then (under some mild conditions on E and F) the contraction ratios of E and F are logarithmically commensurable.  This provides more evidence for a Conjecture of Feng, Huang, and Rao, that states that these contraction ratios are logarithmically commensurable whenever F admits an affine embedding into E (under some mild conditions).  Our method is a combination of an argument based on the approach of Feng, Huang, and Rao, with a new result by Hochman, which is related to the increase of entropy of measures under convolutions."
- Relevance: same line (embeddings), not positivity.

### 1.7  Xiao (2024), "On a self-embedding problem for self-similar sets"
- Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 44 (2024), no. 10, 3002–3011.  Opened: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ergodic-theory-and-dynamical-systems/article/on-a-selfembedding-problem-for-selfsimilar-sets/A804BD23FA79328818C0A7AF6B71C322 .
- Abstract (verbatim): "Let K ⊂ ℝ^d be a self-similar set generated by an iterated function system {φ_i}_{i=1}^m satisfying the strong separation condition and let f be a contracting similitude with f(K) ⊆ K.  We show that f(K) is relatively open in K if all φ_i share a common contraction ratio and orthogonal part.  We also provide a counterexample when the orthogonal parts are allowed to vary.  This partially answers a question of Elekes, Keleti and Máthé [Ergod. Th. & Dynam. Sys. 30 (2010), 399–440].  As a byproduct of our argument, when d = 1 and K admits two homogeneous generating iterated function systems satisfying the strong separation condition but with contraction ratios of opposite signs, we show that K is symmetric.  This partially answers a question of Feng and Wang [Adv. Math. 222 (2009), 1964–1981]."
- Relevance: embeddings only.

### 1.8  Furstenberg (2008), "Ergodic fractal measures and dimension conservation"
- Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 28 (2008), 405–422.  DOI 10.1017/s0143385708000084.  Opened: OpenAlex record https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Ergodic+fractal+measures+and+dimension+conservation .
- Abstract (verbatim): "A linear map from one Euclidean space to another may map a compact set bijectively to a set of smaller Hausdorff dimension.  For 'homogeneous' fractals (to be defined), there is a phenomenon of 'dimension conservation'.  In proving this we shall introduce dynamical systems whose states represent compactly supported measures in which progression in time corresponds to progressively increasing magnification.  Application of the ergodic theorem will show that, generically, dimension conservation is valid.  This 'almost everywhere' result implies a non-probabilistic statement for homogeneous fractals."
- Relevance (analysis): K_d × K_d is homogeneous, and K ∩ (λK + t) is the fibre over t of the projection (x,y) ↦ x − λy.  Dimension conservation therefore guarantees fibres of dimension ≥ 2·dim K − 1 > 0 over SOME t, but says nothing about the specific fibre t = 0 (or t ∈ ℤ), which is our object.

### 1.9  Wu (2019), Shmerkin (2019): Furstenberg's intersection conjecture (different bases only)

### 1.10  Peres, Shmerkin (2009), "Resonance between Cantor sets"
- Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 29 (2009), 201–221; arXiv:0705.2628.  Opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.2628 .
- Abstract (verbatim): "Let C_a be the central Cantor set obtained by removing a central interval of length 1−2a from the unit interval, and continuing this process inductively on each of the remaining two intervals.  We prove that if log b/log a is irrational, then dim(C_a + C_b) = min(dim(C_a) + dim(C_b), 1), where dim is Hausdorff dimension."
- Relevance: sums, not intersections, and the theorem is for the non-resonant (irrational log-ratio) case; the same-base case is the "resonant" one, which is exactly ours.

### 2.7  Lagarias (2009), "Ternary expansions of powers of 2"

### 2.8  Abram, Lagarias (2014), "Intersections of multiplicative translates of 3-adic Cantor sets"
- J. Fractal Geom. 1 (2014), no. 4, 349–390.  arXiv:1308.3133.  Opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3133 and https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1308.3133 .
- Abstract (verbatim): "Motivated by a question of Erdős, this paper considers questions concerning the discrete dynamical system on the 3-adic integers given by multiplication by 2.  Let the 3-adic Cantor set consist of all 3-adic integers whose expansions use only the digits 0 and 1.  The exception set is the set of 3-adic integers whose forward orbits under this action intersects the 3-adic Cantor set infinitely many times.  It has been shown that this set has Hausdorff dimension 0.  Approaches to upper bounds on the Hausdorff dimensions of these sets leads to study of intersections of multiplicative translates of Cantor sets by powers of 2.  More generally, this paper studies the structure of finite intersections of general multiplicative translates of the 3-adic Cantor set by integers 1 < M_1 < M_2 < … < M_n.  These sets are describable as sets of 3-adic integers whose 3-adic expansions have one-sided symbolic dynamics given by a finite automaton.  As a consequence, the Hausdorff dimension of such a set is always of the form log(β) for an algebraic integer β.  This paper gives a method to determine the automaton for given data (M_1, …, M_n).  Experimental results indicate that the Hausdorff dimension of such sets depends in a very complicated way on the integers M_1, …, M_n."
- Notation: 𝒞(1, M_1, …, M_n) = Σ_{3,2} ∩ (1/M_1)Σ_{3,2} ∩ … , i.e. 3-adic integers x with x, M_1 x, …, M_n x all digit-{0,1}.
- Theorem 1.6 (verbatim from ar5iv): "(1) There is a terminating algorithm that takes as input any finite set of integers 1 ≤ M_1 < … < M_n, and gives as output a labeled directed graph 𝒢 = (G, ℒ) with a marked starting vertex v_0, which is a presentation of a path set X = X(1, M_1, M_2, ⋯, M_n) describing the 3-adic expansions of the elements of the space 𝒞(1, M_1, …, M_n).  (2) The topological entropy β of the path set X is the Perron eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix A of the directed graph G.  It is a real algebraic integer satisfying 1 ≤ β ≤ 2.  Furthermore the Hausdorff dimension dim_H(𝒞(1, M_1, …, M_n)) = log_3 β."
- Theorem 1.7(2),(3) (verbatim): for L_k = (1^k)_3, "dim_H(𝒞(1, L_k)) = log_3 β_k, where β_k is the unique real root greater than 1 of λ^k − λ^{k−1} − 1 = 0" and "For all k ≥ 3, dim_H(𝒞(1, L_k)) = log_3 k / k + O(log log(k)/k)."
- Theorem 1.8(2) (verbatim): for N_k = 3^k + 1, "dim_H(𝒞(1, N_k)) = log_3((1+√5)/2) ≈ 0.438018."
- Theorem 4.1 (verbatim): "(1) If the smallest nonzero 3-adic digit in the 3-adic expansion of the positive integer M is 2, then dim_H(𝒞(1,M)) = 0.  (2) If positive integers M_1, M_2, …, M_n all have 3-adic expansions containing only digits 0 and 1, then dim_H(𝒞(1, M_1, M_2, …, M_n)) > 0."
- Relevance: THIS is the integer/p-adic form of item 2 and of the "λ-multiplier automaton" in item 4, for base 3 and digit set {0,1}: the intersection is a path set (automaton), the dimension is log_3 of a Perron eigenvalue, positivity holds for a named class of M (digits in {0,1}), vanishing holds for another named class (lowest nonzero digit 2), and the general dependence is called "very complicated".  A truncation of a 3-adic point of 𝒞(1,M) gives an integer B with B and MB digit-{0,1} up to the top carry, so exponential growth of such B is equivalent to positive dimension here.

## 4.  Is K_d ∩ λK_d automatic/sofic, with dimension = log of a spectral radius?

### 4.1  Boigelot, Rassart, Wolper (1998) via Boigelot, Brusten, Bruyère (2010)
- Boigelot, Brusten, Bruyère, "On the sets of real numbers recognized by finite automata in multiple bases", Logical Methods in Computer Science 6(1) (2010); arXiv:1001.2508.  Opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.2508 and the PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.2508 (text extracted with pdftotext).
- Theorem 2.1 ([BRW98]) (verbatim from the PDF): "Let r ∈ ℕ_{>1} be a base.  A subset of ℝ is r-recognizable iff it is definable in the first-order theory ⟨ℝ, ℤ, +, <, X_r⟩, where X_r(x,u,k) is a base-dependent predicate that holds whenever u is an integer power of r, and there exists an encoding of x in which the digit at the position specified by u is equal to k."  ([BRW98] = B. Boigelot, S. Rassart, P. Wolper, "On the expressiveness of real and integer arithmetic automata", Proc. 25th ICALP, LNCS 1443 (1998), 152–163; the ICALP page itself was behind a Springer redirect and was not opened.)
- Consequence (analysis, not a quote): K_d = {x ∈ [0,1] : ∀u (power of g) ¬X_g(x,u,d) for the relevant encoding} is g-recognizable, and {x : x ∈ K_d ∧ ∃y (q·y = p·x ∧ y ∈ K_d)} is a first-order formula in that language (multiplication by the constants p, q is iterated addition), hence K_d ∩ (p/q)K_d is g-recognizable, i.e. accepted by a Büchi automaton reading base-g expansions.  Since it is also closed, its set of digit sequences is a closed ω-regular set; its language of finite blocks is regular, so the mod-1 version (which is ×g-invariant) is a sofic subshift.  Nondeterminism (the carry guessed from the left) is inherent; a determinized right-resolving presentation exists by standard sofic-shift theory.

### 4.2  Furstenberg (1967): dimension of a closed ×m-invariant set = entropy / log m
- Cited by Kenyon–Peres–Solomyak, "Hausdorff dimension of the multiplicative golden mean shift" (arXiv:1105.3441), whose introduction was opened at https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1105.3441 .  Quoted there (verbatim): "if X is a closed subset of [0,1], invariant under the map T_m : x ↦ mx (mod 1), then its Hausdorff dimension equals the Minkowski (box-counting) dimension, which equals the topological entropy of T_m|_X divided by log m", with citation "[5] H. Furstenberg.  Disjointness in ergodic theory, minimal sets, and a problem in Diophantine approximation.  Math. Systems Theory 1 (1967), 1–49."
- Consequence (analysis): for the mod-1 version of K_d ∩ λK_d (closed, ×g-invariant, sofic), dim_H = h_top/log g = log(Perron eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of a right-resolving presentation)/log g.  The entropy-equals-log-spectral-radius step is textbook sofic-shift theory (Lind–Marcus, Ch. 4); not separately fetched.

### 4.3  Abram–Lagarias (2014) [2.8], Theorem 1.6
- The p-adic statement of exactly this: an explicit automaton for 𝒞(1, M_1, …, M_n), dimension log_3 of its Perron eigenvalue, computed for infinite families.  For the real line, [2.10] warns that the closure properties "do not hold over the real numbers", which is about the class of path-set fractals under p-adic arithmetic, not about recognizability, and does not contradict 4.1.

### 4.4  Adamczewski, Bell (2011), "An analogue of Cobham's theorem for fractals"
- Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 363 (2011), 4421–4442.  Located via WebSearch (author PDF at https://adamczewski.perso.math.cnrs.fr/Fractals_New.pdf , not opened).  Search summary only: introduces k-self-similar compact sets (those whose base-k expansions are recognized by an automaton), shows the triadic Cantor set, carpet, sponge are k-self-similar, and proves a Cobham theorem: a set that is both k- and ℓ-self-similar for multiplicatively independent k, ℓ is a finite union of rational intervals. ## Verdict (my analysis)

1. **"dim(K_d ∩ λK_d) > 0 for every rational λ (coprime to g)" is not a known theorem, not a stated conjecture, and is FALSE in general.**  Hand derivation for g = 3: with d = 1 (K_1 = C = digits {0,2}) and λ = 2, x ∈ C ∩ 2C means x ∈ C and x/2 ∈ C; but x/2 = ∑ (a_i/2) 3^{−i} is a valid expansion with digits in {0,1}, so x/2 ∈ C forces a double expansion, giving C ∩ 2C = {0} ∪ {2·3^{−j} : j ≥ 1}.  The mod-1 fibre x ∈ C, (x+1)/2 ∈ C is countable by the same argument with digits {1,2}.  For d = 2 (digits {0,1}) the map y ↦ 2y sends K_2 digitwise onto K_1, so K_2 ∩ 2K_2 is countable as well; and for d = 0 (digits {1,2} ⊂ [1/2,1]) write α = 1/2 + γ with γ ∈ K_2, so frac(2α) = 2γ ∈ K_1, and K_1 ∩ K_0 is countable.  Hence in base 3 the multiplier pair (1,2) hits every digit d for every irrational α, with either convention.  This is the real-line twin of Abram–Lagarias Theorem 4.1(1) (3-adic: lowest nonzero digit of M equal to 2 ⇒ dimension 0), and it means the base-4 phenomenon you observe (every coprime pair below 500 fails to hit some digit) cannot be an instance of an all-bases theorem; if true it is base-specific (three allowed digits leave room for carries that two do not).

2. **What IS known** is the upper side.  Jiang–Li–Li–Wu (arXiv:2607.19813, July 2026) prove for missing-digit sets and rational γ coprime to the base that dim̄_M((γK+α) ∩ K) < dim K for all α (with an explicit bound), and for prime base and symmetric digit set that this happens iff γ ∉ ±b^ℤ; Elekes–Keleti–Máthé + Feng–Wang give the measure-zero statement.  The only lower-bound result found is their one-line example dim(4C ∩ C) ≥ log2/log9.

3. **The integer version** (B and MB both missing a digit) is treated only in the 3-adic form by Lagarias (2009) and Abram–Lagarias (2014, 2017): finite automaton, dimension log_3 of a Perron eigenvalue, positivity for M with 3-adic digits in {0,1}, vanishing when the lowest nonzero digit is 2, and "very complicated" dependence otherwise.  Nothing for general base g or for two multipliers on the real line; EMS I/II, Konyagin, Dartyge–Mauduit, Maynard are about residue classes, prime factors, almost primes and primes, not about multiples.

4. **Sofic/automatic**: yes, and the dimension is log(spectral radius)/log g, but by assembly of standard results (BRW98 recognizability + Furstenberg 1967 dimension = entropy/log g + sofic entropy = log Perron eigenvalue), not by a paper devoted to K_d ∩ λK_d.  Kenyon 1997 and Liu–Xi–Zhao 2007 are the worked real-line templates; Abram–Lagarias is the worked p-adic template.

5. **Closest paper**: Jiang–Li–Li–Wu 2026 for the real-line statement (and its Corollary 1.6 is the right upper-side complement to any positivity conjecture); Abram–Lagarias 2014 for the automaton/spectral-radius mechanism and for the only existing positivity/vanishing criteria (base 3, p-adic).

6. **Status of your base-4 observation**: as far as this search shows, unstudied.  A precise conjecture would have to be base-aware, e.g. "for g ≥ 4 and every rational λ = p/q with gcd(pq, g) = 1 there is a digit d with dim(K_d ∩ λK_d) > 0", and the automaton of §4.1 makes each instance decidable (the intersection has positive dimension iff some nontrivial strongly connected component is reachable from the initial carry state).
