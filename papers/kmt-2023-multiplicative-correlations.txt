2.2. Hudson’s conjecture. Motivated by the question of the longest consecutive runs
of quadratic residues, we say that a completely multiplicative function f : N → {−1, +1}
has length ℓ if ℓ is the maximal number of consecutive +1’s that occur in the sequence
(f (n))n≥1 . That is, f has length ℓ if there exists m ∈ N such that

                  (f (m), f (m + 1), . . . , f (m + ℓ − 1)) = (+1, +1, . . . , +1),

but for any n ∈ N,

                       (f (n), f (n + 1), . . . , f (n + ℓ)) ̸= (+1, . . . , +1).

We denote by F2,ℓ the set of all completely multiplicative f : N → {−1, 1} of length ℓ. In
1962, D.H. Lehmer and E. Lehmer [31] noted (and later Mills conjectured [36]) that F2,2
consists of two functions, f3+ and f3− , defined by complete multiplicativity as follows: for
p ̸= 3 we set f3+ (p) = f3− (p) = p3 , and for p = 3 we set f3+ (3) = −f3− (3) = +1. That
is, f3± are defined by taking the Legendre symbol modulo 3 and modifying the value at
its conductor, 3, so that it takes values in {−1, +1}. This conjecture was later proved by
Schur [38].
   Almost fifty years ago, Hudson [22] considered the much more difficult classification
problem for F2,3 , i.e., completely multiplicative ±1-valued functions with length 3. He
conjectured the following:

non-pretentious multiplicative function. Then it has a unique Furstenberg system, which
