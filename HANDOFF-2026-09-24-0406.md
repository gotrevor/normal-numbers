## 🧠 Context to carry forward

**The base-6 tail bound does not need logarithms.** The forced-zero argument needs
`2·6^p < 2^{3^{m+1}}`, which reads as `1.1·log₂6 ≈ 2.84 < 3`. Don't import `Real.log`: raise
both sides to the 10th power, so the transcendental collapses to `6^10 = 60466176 < 67108864
= 2^26` (one `norm_num`) and the rest is `10 + 26p ≤ 30·3^m` by `omega`. The whole statement
stays in ℕ. That is `two_mul_six_pow_lt_two_pow`, and the trick generalizes — pick the
rational approximation's denominator to match the accuracy you need, with slack.

**Why the abelian separation actually works** (this is the part that isn't obvious from the
file and took the whole lap to see). `hexSwap` (2→3, 5→4, B→A, C→D) is **not injective** —
both `2` and `3` map to `3`. So the nibble distribution is genuinely non-uniform, and that
non-uniformity is precisely what kills normality (`0011` has frequency `5/64`, not `1/16`).
The temptation is to look for an invariance that a bijection would give you; there isn't one.
What survives is sharper and is exactly what abelian normality reads:

> over **every** contiguous bit interval `[lo,hi) ⊆ [0,4)` of a nibble — *the full nibble
> included* — the one-count of `hexSwap d` is Binomial(`hi−lo`, 1/2) as `d` ranges over
> `{0,…,15}`.

Two different reasons for the two cases: proper intervals because each swap moves a digit
inside its own 2-bit **quadrant** *and* its own 2-bit **residue class**; the full nibble
because the swaps permute popcounts `{1,2}→{2,2}`, `{1,2}→{1,1}`, `{2,3}→{2,2}`, `{2,3}→{3,3}`,
preserving the multiset `{1,1,2,2,2,2,3,3}`. Once stated that way it is 125 `(lo,hi,t)`
triples and **one `decide`** (`nibCount_binomial`). Everything else is bookkeeping: a window
is a contiguous interval of the first nibble, then whole nibbles, then a prefix of the last,
the nibbles independent by base-16 normality of `c`, and Chu-Vandermonde convolves them.

Design lesson worth keeping: to break normality while preserving an abelian statistic, reach
for a **non-bijective** substitution that preserves the statistic's marginals.
